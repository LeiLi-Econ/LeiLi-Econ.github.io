This is an essay I wrote in Chinese in July 2025. I was on my gap year, having been recommended for admission to the M.A. programme at the School of Economics and Management (SEM), Nanchang University. I wanted to learn some basic macro-modelling skills and to organize my observations and thoughts about the economic environment around me, so I wrote this essay to share and discuss those thoughts with my friends. 

I started from a cultural observation: long before the price indexes turned, the mood around us had already changed — anti-involution sentiment, fatigue with meritocracy, anxiety, and a vague sense that the days of "getting better" were over. I wanted to ask where that mood came from, and I ended up with an economics essay. The answer I reached is that China's deflation since the third quarter of 2022 is a structural crisis: a growth path built on the demographic dividend and on export- and investment-led development, the systematic suppression of labor compensation and public services, and the intertwined expansion of land finance and local implicit debt. These factors together produced a weak consumption base, and the real estate financial bubble turned it into a deflationary spiral.

The essay itself admits that it contains no original contribution; it is an exercise in sorting out an argument and practicing some economic tools — a simple analytical model, a fixed-effects regression on 77 economies from 2011 to 2021, and a three-sector New Keynesian DSGE simulation with heterogeneous households. I keep that framing here. I have not updated the analysis; it reflects what I believed in July 2025.

## Abstract

China's prolonged downturn that began in the third quarter of 2022 has been marked by low inflation and can be identified as a deflationary crisis, with wide-ranging social consequences that point to deeper systemic problems. This essay argues that the structural factors behind the current deflation are a growth path long dependent on the demographic dividend, a development model led by exports and investment, the systematic suppression of labor compensation and public services, and the intertwined expansion of land finance and local implicit debt, which together produced a weak consumption base and chronically insufficient domestic demand. Using cross-country data on 77 economies from 2011 to 2021, the essay empirically tests the effect of the distribution structure on deflation risk; the regression results support the hypothesis that distributional imbalance induces economic contraction. The essay also builds a simplified three-sector New Keynesian DSGE model with heterogeneous households to simulate the response of the price level to an improvement in the distribution structure, and finds that raising the economic position of middle- and low-income groups helps relieve deflationary pressure. Finally, the essay discusses policy paths that operate through distributional adjustment and points to directions for further thought. This essay is dedicated to the dull life of an age of recession.

## Résumé

La longue récession de l'économie chinoise amorcée au troisième trimestre 2022, marquée par une faible inflation, peut être identifiée comme une crise déflationniste, dont les conséquences sociales très larges révèlent des problèmes systémiques plus profonds. Cet essai soutient que les facteurs structurels de cette déflation sont un sentier de croissance longtemps dépendant du dividende démographique, un modèle de développement dominé par les exportations et l'investissement, la compression systématique de la rémunération du travail et des services publics, ainsi que l'expansion enchevêtrée de la finance foncière et de la dette implicite des gouvernements locaux — autant de facteurs qui ont produit une base de consommation fragile et une insuffisance chronique de la demande intérieure. À partir de données internationales portant sur 77 économies de 2011 à 2021, l'essai teste empiriquement l'effet de la structure de répartition sur le risque de déflation ; les résultats de régression confirment l'hypothèse selon laquelle le déséquilibre distributif induit une contraction économique. L'essai construit également un modèle DSGE néo-keynésien à trois secteurs avec ménages hétérogènes afin de simuler la réponse du niveau des prix à une amélioration de la structure de répartition, et constate qu'élever la position économique des groupes à revenus moyens et faibles contribue à atténuer les pressions déflationnistes. Enfin, l'essai discute des voies de politique économique passant par l'ajustement distributif et suggère des directions de réflexion. Cet essai est dédié à la vie terne d'un âge de récession.
