This paper studies the carbon-neutrality dividend of regional digital infrastructure. Exploiting the Broadband China pilot policy as a quasi-natural experiment in a multi-period difference-in-differences framework, and using panel data from 291 Chinese prefecture-level cities over 2008–2021, it finds that digital infrastructure significantly promotes carbon-neutralization innovation, innovation in carbon-intensive industries, and carbon-emission efficiency in pilot cities. The results are robust to propensity score matching, coarsened exact matching, alternative estimators, and instrumental-variable regressions. Mechanism analysis shows that digital infrastructure advances carbon neutrality by rationalizing the industrial structure, fostering the digital economy, and inducing digital-technology innovation, with a larger dividend in cities with stronger economic development, lower resource dependence, and weaker environmental constraints.
